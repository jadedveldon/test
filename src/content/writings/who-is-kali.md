Kali is the Devi in her most fearless form. Her name is linked to *kāla*, which means both time and black: she is the power that brings everything into being and, in its season, takes it back. For those who love her, that is not a threat but a promise. Whatever binds you is also held by time, and Kali is the one who loosens it.

## Reading her image

Every part of Kali's form is a teaching.

- **Her dark skin** is the dark before creation, the night from which all colour comes. Nothing is outside her.
- **The garland** of heads she wears is explained in Tantric teaching as the letters of the Sanskrit alphabet (fifty, or fifty-one by some counts), the root of every word and thought. She wears language itself; she is beyond it.
- **The sword** cuts through ignorance and false identity. It is the clean cut that frees.
- **Her right hands**, in the well-known Dakshina Kali image, make the gestures of "do not fear" (abhaya) and of giving blessings (varada). The fierce form is turned towards protecting you.
- **Standing on Shiva**, she is pure power resting on pure awareness. Neither is complete without the other.

## Kali in Bengal

Kali is especially beloved in Bengal, where she is worshipped as Ma, the Mother. The great temples of Kalighat and Dakshineswar in Kolkata, and the poet-saint Ramprasad, who sang to her as a child sings to their mother, shaped a devotion that is tender as much as it is fierce.

Her great night is Kali Puja, on the new moon (Amavasya) of the month of Kartik, and new-moon nights are widely kept for her.

## Coming to her

People come to Kali when something needs to end: a fear, a pattern, a binding, a grief that will not move. They also come simply to be held by something bigger than their story. Sarvakali's work with Kali can be a single conversation, a ritual on Amavasya, or a longer practice.
