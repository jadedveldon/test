Kali is the Devi in her most fearless form. Her name comes from *kala*, time: she is the power that brings everything into being and, in its season, takes it back. For those who love her, that is not a threat but a promise. Whatever binds you is also held by time, and Kali is the one who loosens it.

## Reading her image

Every part of Kali's form is a teaching.

- **Her dark skin** is the dark before creation, the night from which all colour comes. Nothing is outside her.
- **The garland** she wears is often explained as the fifty letters of the Sanskrit alphabet, the root of every word and thought. She wears language itself; she is beyond it.
- **The sword** cuts through ignorance and false identity. It is the clean cut that frees.
- **Her tongue** and her wild hair are energy that has not been tamed into politeness.
- **Her raised right hand** says "do not fear", and her lower right hand offers blessings. The fierce form is turned towards protecting you.
- **Standing on Shiva**, she is pure power resting on pure awareness. Neither is complete without the other.

## Kali in Bengal

Much of the way Kali is worshipped today comes from Bengal, where she is loved as Ma, the Mother. The great temples of Kalighat and Dakshineswar in Kolkata, and the poet-saint Ramprasad, who sang to her as a child sings to their mother, shaped a devotion that is tender as much as it is fierce.

Her great night is Kali Puja, on the new moon (Amavasya) of the month of Kartik. Every Amavasya is sacred to her.

## Coming to her

People come to Kali when something needs to end: a fear, a pattern, a binding, a grief that will not move. They also come simply to be held by something bigger than their story. Sarvakali's work with Kali can be a single conversation, a ritual on Amavasya, or a longer practice.
